# Evidence-led literature review

A useful literature review begins with an explicit question and inclusion
criteria. Record the source and location of every material claim. Distinguish a
reported result from an inference made by the reviewer.

First, extract the methods, population, intervention, comparison, and measured
outcomes from each paper. Then compare assumptions and study limitations before
combining conclusions. Never hide contradictory evidence. If the available
sources do not resolve a question, report the uncertainty and identify the
minimum additional evidence required.

A final review should be checked by a second responsibility: list each material
claim, trace it to evidence, flag unsupported generalization, and verify that
the conclusion stays within the scope of the cited studies.
